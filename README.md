# Decimal Dungeon

Download the [latest release](https://github.com/DanielRLowe/Decimal-Dungeon/releases/latest).

On **Windows 10 or 11 (x64)**, run **Decimal-Dungeon.exe**. It opens the game in your default browser. Keep the launcher window open while playing. Progress is saved in that browser profile on this computer.

On **macOS**, unzip **Decimal-Dungeon-macOS.zip**. Double-click **Decimal Dungeon.app** on an Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 13 or later. All the art, game code, and math challenges travel inside the app. It works offline and saves progress on the Mac.

## Start your empire

Open **Campaign**, choose a **Campaign era**, and pick a real country as your home. Choose the modern world, the eve of World War II in 1939, the eve of World War I in 1914, or the world of 1886. Changing eras saves your place. Each map keeps its own home, rivals, conquered countries, capital edits and unfinished battles. Your hero and lesson journal travel across eras. On the political map, inspect a neighbor to see its guardian and which countries you could reach by taking it. Marked sea routes let your empire reach islands and cross oceans. Pick a target to start a country battle. During a battle or a practice lesson, the small **Campaign map** shows the current era and each empire’s territory. Click it to open the full map.

Each completed math problem deals damage to the guardian. During decimal column practice, line up the decimal points, fill empty places with zeros, work each column from right to left, and check your answer with an estimate. Other lessons use their own written steps, diagrams and checks. You can ask for hints or retry a tricky step. A regular or elite campaign guardian falls after one correct problem. A boss takes up to two.

Winning the battle adds the country to your empire and plays a six-second victory film set in its battlefield. Choose **Skip film** to move on or **Replay victory** to watch it again. With Reduce Motion enabled, the film plays when you choose replay. Press **Return** on the result to start the next problem. After a conquest, the highlighted button names the next country. Choose **See your empire** to pick a different target on the map. Bring every playable country under your banner to win the world campaign. Your borders, unfinished battles, and math work save on this Mac.

After choosing your home, open **Manage empires** on the campaign map to name your empire and add up to three computer rivals. Pick a name and a home country for each. Rivals take a turn when you capture a country. Watch the standings and colored borders to see which empire reaches the territory goal first. You can also win by capturing every rival holding. You can continue conquering after the race.

On the map, select any country you own and choose **Manage country**. Rename the country or its capital, choose a capital site from the list, or use **Place on map** to pick a spot on that country's land. Check **Make this country the empire seat** to move the seat of government there. Choose **Save changes**. The map search recognizes both the original and custom names. Clear a name to restore the original, or select **Original capital site** to move the city back.

On the modern map, the 195 playable countries include all 193 UN members, the Holy See (Vatican City), and the State of Palestine. Other land provides geographic context. The coastlines and borders use Natural Earth's public-domain 1:50 million country outlines. The capital markers use Natural Earth populated places with updates for newer capitals, and are approximate at this map scale. [Natural Earth populated places](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-populated-places/)

Sea routes are game connections, and guardians and battle landscapes are fantasy artwork. [Natural Earth country data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-cultural-vectors/50m-admin-0-countries-2/) · [UN member-state roster](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4082085)

The three historical maps use dated borders and capitals from CShapes 2.0. They group dependencies with the governing state recorded for that date. Some early borders are approximate. The 1939 snapshot follows the source’s treatment of recognized sovereignty, which leaves out some wartime occupations. Expand **Historical map notes and credits** below the era selector for context. [Historical atlas credits and license](web/historical-atlas-credits.html) · [ETH Zürich CShapes](https://icr.ethz.ch/data/cshapes/)

## Explore a country

Click a country on the map or find it in **Inspect country**. Its population appears beneath **Country profile**. Open the profile to see the flag when available, locate the capital, and read about the country before choosing a home or a battle. A renamed country keeps its real identity in the atlas. The profiles work offline. Select a source link to read more in your browser.

Modern profiles include population, surface area, GDP in current US dollars, and armed forces personnel. Each figure shows its observation year. The personnel series measures active service across military branches, including qualifying paramilitary personnel. The most recent figure can be several years old. For 173 countries the profile also features a World Heritage Site with a link to its UNESCO page. [World Bank data](https://data.worldbank.org/) · [UN country profiles](https://data.un.org/legacy/en/index.html) · [UNESCO World Heritage List](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/)

Historical profiles use estimates for that map’s year. Military personnel counts refer to January 1. GDP, where available, is expressed in 2011 international dollars, a unit that accounts for differences in buying power. Notes identify the territory covered when a statistic describes only part of an empire. The game labels gaps in the records and displays a period flag where its design could be verified. [Correlates of War, National Material Capabilities](https://correlatesofwar.org/data-sets/national-material-capabilities/) · [Maddison Project Database 2023](https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023) · [Historical figures and flag sources](web/historical-country-sources.html)

## Choose a lesson

Open **Lessons** to pick from 37 math lessons. Choose **Foundation**, **Core** or **Challenge**, then choose **Practice**. Every lesson and difficulty is available immediately. The strip above the battlefield names the active lesson, session and difficulty. **Tutorial** opens a worked example and returns you to the same step. **Change lesson** opens the library from a battle.

The grade-five lessons cover the five strands in the Tennessee standards linked by Knox County Schools. Search by title, topic or standard code, such as **5.NF.B.4**. The examples and practice problems were written for this game. [Knox County Schools elementary instruction](https://www.knoxschools.org/studentsfamilies/instruction/esinstruction) · [Tennessee grade-five mathematics standards](https://bestforall-cms.tnedu.gov/sites/default/files/documents/G5_9-24-21.pdf)

| Topic | What he can work on |
| --- | --- |
| Expressions and patterns | Parentheses, order of operations, translating stories into expressions, generating two patterns and graphing their ordered pairs |
| Base ten and decimals | Place value, powers of ten, reading and comparing decimals, rounding on a number line, whole-number multiplication and division, and all four decimal operations |
| Fractions | Equivalent fractions, unlike denominators, mixed numbers, fair shares, multiplication and scaling, area models, and division by fractional serving sizes |
| Measurement and data | Customary and metric conversions, time and money, fraction line plots, unit cubes, rectangular prisms, and additive volume |
| Geometry | Plotting points, interpreting coordinates in context, and classifying shapes by their properties |

Open **Progress** to see every lesson grouped by topic, including unstarted, in-progress and mastered lessons. A mastery mark is earned by finishing an original Core or Challenge problem independently, with every step correct on the first try and without hints. Three marks complete that lesson. Foundation practice, extra problems after mastery and repeat reviews are also tracked. **Extra practice** lets him revisit a mastered skill at any difficulty.

Country battles use the **Grade 5 mastery path** by default. The country dossier shows the next skill and its mastery count. For example, three independent place-value solutions move the path to powers of ten, even if those wins came in different countries or eras. Hints and retries still let him conquer the country. Math mastery carries between campaigns. Inspect a frontier and use **Choose a lesson** above the map to bring a particular lesson into that battle. Choose **Grade 5 mastery path** again to resume the sequence. **Classic decimal campaign** selects the original arithmetic progression based on the size of the empire. A battle already in progress resumes with its saved problem.

For school-style addition, choose **Add three decimals in columns** or **Choose a pair and add in two stages**. In the paired version, choose any two numbers, align and pad their columns, and enter each digit and carry from right to left. Write the subtotal and repeat with the third number. Finish with an estimate. **Show my work** displays the two completed additions. For example, pairing 1.75 with 2.25 first leaves 4 + 0.625 = 4.625.

## Work through a problem

In **Add unlike fractions** and **Subtract unlike fractions**, round each amount on its number line, then find a common denominator. To write 4 3/4 in twelfths, split each quarter into three pieces. Tap the four whole bars to count 12, 24, 36, 48. Tap the three shaded quarters to count 3, 6, 9. Choose **Check piece counts**, then work out 48 + 9 and enter your total. When your sum is correct, it fills the numerator above the 12. Press Return or choose **Submit numerator** to advance. For an amount smaller than one whole, the checked count of shaded pieces fills the numerator directly. Earlier pictures are available from **View work**.

**Math paper** opens next to the problem. Choose **Draw** to write with a trackpad, mouse or finger, or **Type** for keyboard working. The paper stays with the problem when switching sessions or closing the game. You can reopen it from the result or the saved problem in Sessions.

The quick-solve clock counts active solving time. Fully working a problem within its time target earns a **critical hit**, which doubles attack damage and adds bonus XP equal to the attack’s original power. Targets allow more time for longer problems. Regular battle rewards and mastery rules still apply when you take longer. The clock pauses on the map, in tutorials and while the game is in the background. Use **Game settings** to turn timed bonuses on or off.

Turn on **Music** to hear *Atlas at Dawn* on the campaign map and during regular practice. Country battles play *Lanterns Across the Vale*, a slower, softer orchestral theme for working through a problem. The music pauses for victory films. After a conquest or a return to the map, *Atlas at Dawn* resumes. Both scores play offline, and the game remembers your music switch separately from battle sound effects.

## Return to previous work

Open **Sessions** to continue a lesson, resume a particular unfinished problem, inspect the checked work, or **Practice again** with the same original numbers. Review also remembers the step where you stopped. Each original problem awards its battle rewards once. The **Parent progress report** includes earlier totals, progress by lesson, the learning settings and the local parent PIN.

## Move it to his Mac

1. Transfer **Decimal Dungeon.zip** to his MacBook, then double-click the ZIP.
2. Drag **Decimal Dungeon.app** to Applications and double-click it.
3. If macOS says it cannot verify the developer, open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** after the first launch attempt. Choose **Open Anyway** for Decimal Dungeon and confirm. This personal build is signed locally.

Each Mac user has their own adventure. To bring an existing save to another Mac, quit the game on both machines. In Finder choose **Go → Go to Folder** and enter `~/Library/Application Support/Decimal Dungeon/`. Copy `progress.plist` to the same folder on the other Mac. Replacing that file replaces the destination adventure and its parent PIN.

## Rebuild from source

On Apple Silicon with Xcode Command Line Tools, run `python3 scripts/build-mac.py` from this folder. The build script compiles the native launcher, embeds the contents of `web/`, signs the app locally, and verifies a copy extracted from the transfer archive.

For a reproducible native playtest, run `python3 scripts/build-mac.py --playtest`. It exercises the campaign and math workbench using temporary saves, and launches the packaged app through macOS. The math, adventure, and campaign tests run with `node --test tests/*.test.cjs`.

Build the standalone Windows executable with Go 1.20 or later from the repository root. On macOS or Linux run `GOOS=windows GOARCH=amd64 CGO_ENABLED=0 go build -trimpath -o Decimal-Dungeon.exe windows-launcher.go`. The launcher embeds the web game and serves it only on the local loopback interface.
