Borders and political status

Guy Schvitz, Seraina Rüegger, Luc Girardin, Lars-Erik Cederman, Nils B. Weidmann and Kristian Skrede Gleditsch created CShapes 2.0. The game uses the Gleditsch–Ward version of the dataset distributed by ETH Zurich. Each snapshot selects the records in force on the stated date. The archive includes geometries, period dates, capital coordinates, political status and governing-state identifiers. CShapes data and documentation · Research article in the Journal of Conflict Resolution

CShapes follows recognized territorial changes. Some occupations never changed its boundaries. For example, the 1939 map shows Austria, Czechoslovakia and Ethiopia with their CShapes outlines even though military events had already changed control on the ground. Areas absent from the source appear as pale land. Political communities and people also lived in these areas. Dataset coding rules

The adaptation groups colonial possessions and protectorates under their recorded governing power, while dominions that CShapes lists separately stay playable. Each country’s map includes its recorded holdings across the world. In 1886 the Congo Free State appears as a separate possession of Leopold II. It became a Belgian colony in 1908. Royal Museum for Central Africa

Capital names and markers

Capital markers normally use CShapes coordinates. A few coastal markers move slightly inland to fit the scale of the artwork. The atlas uses period-specific state and city names. In Bolivia, Sucre appears in 1886, and La Paz represents the seat of government in the later maps. United Nations country profile · Diplomatic records from the Federal Revolution

  • Fez was Morocco’s political capital before the transfer to Rabat in 1912. UNESCO World Heritage Centre
  • Yohannes IV established his imperial seat in Mekelle, the Ethiopian marker in 1886. UN-Habitat profile of Mekelle
  • Norway’s capital was called Kristiania in 1914. The city changed its name to Oslo in 1925. Oslo City Archives
  • Chongqing served as China’s wartime seat in August 1939. U.S. Embassy dispatch from May 1939
  • Spain’s government remained at Burgos until October 1939. Spanish Ministry of Defence library

Adaptation and license

Decimal Dungeon selected the snapshots, grouped recorded holdings, simplified shared boundary arcs, projected them for its map, updated display names and selected capital sites, and assigned colors and anchor points. The derived historical atlas is distributed under the same Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial–ShareAlike 4.0 International license as the original CShapes data. Full license terms

The pale background coastline uses Natural Earth’s 1:110m land polygons, released into the public domain. Sea crossings and the 1886 overland expedition between the Red Sea coast and the Ethiopian highlands are game rules. The fantasy guardians belong to the game.
